Ranco Dental CRM — How It All Works

A plain-English walkthrough of the data model: how a patient's visit turns into a consultation, a treatment, a prescription, and a bill — and how every one of those stays attached to that patient's history, permanently.

The idea in one paragraph

Every patient's record starts the moment they walk in and get registered — nothing happens before that. From there, everything a patient goes through is a chain: a consultation (the exam), which leads to a treatment (the actual procedure, run by an assigned doctor), which is made up of individual visits (each sitting, each with its own charge). Prescriptions are written at the consultation or at any visit, and — unlike a typical medical note — they can be revised later, with every earlier version kept, not overwritten. Money moves twice: once per visit as it happens, and once more at the end when the treatment is marked finished, where an invoice sweeps up whatever's still unpaid. All of it — every consultation, treatment, visit, prescription edit, and payment — lives under that one patient's history, viewable as tabs, forever traceable back to who did what and when.

Entities, one by one

What each table is for, in plain terms.

  • STAFF — every person who logs into the system, Admin and Doctor alike, in one table. A role field tells the two apart; specialty applies to doctors (e.g. "Orthodontist").
  • PATIENT — created the instant someone registers at the clinic. This is the moment their history begins — everything downstream (consultations, treatments, visits, billing) hangs off this one record's id.
  • CONSULTATION — the first meeting: a doctor examines the patient and decides what's needed. Flat ₹500 fee. This always happens before a treatment exists — including the second, third, or any later time the same patient comes back for something new. The consulting doctor is also the one who decides the treatment and assigns who performs it.
  • SERVICE — the clinic's price catalog (RCT, filling, cleaning, etc). Admin-managed only.
  • TREATMENT — created once a consultation has decided what's needed. Carries a link back to the consultation it came from, and to whichever doctor currently owns it — which can change mid-way.
  • TREATMENT_HANDOFF — a log entry created every time the doctor on a treatment changes. Records who it moved from, who it moved to, who made the change, when, and optionally why — so the history shows exactly when and why a handoff happened, instead of silently overwriting the doctor field.
  • VISIT — one row per sitting under a treatment. Carries that sitting's charge and its own paid/unpaid status plus payment mode — settled as it happens, not held until the treatment ends.
  • PRESCRIPTION_ENTRY — a doctor's note, tied to either a consultation or a visit, all feeding into one continuous timeline per patient. It's editable now — but only by the doctor who originally wrote it.
  • PRESCRIPTION_VERSION — every time a prescription entry is edited, the prior version is saved here in full, along with who edited it and when. Nothing is ever silently lost.
  • INVOICE — generated when a treatment is marked finished. It only totals the visits that were still unpaid at that point (already-paid visits don't appear again) and captures the payment mode used to settle them.

A patient's journey, step by step

  1. Patient arrives and registers. A PATIENT row is created — this is when their history begins.
  2. Consultation happens. The doctor examines them, charges the flat ₹500 fee (tracked as paid/unpaid + payment mode), and decides what treatment is needed and who should perform it. A prescription can be written right here if needed (e.g. a painkiller).
  3. Treatment is created. Linked back to that consultation, assigned to a doctor, status "ongoing".
  4. Visits happen. Each sitting has its own charge, its own prescription entry, and gets paid whenever the patient actually pays.
  5. If needed, the doctor changes mid-treatment — logged as a handoff (from whom, to whom, by whom, when, why).
  6. Treatment is marked finished. "Generate Invoice" totals up whatever visits are still unpaid, asks for the payment mode, creates the invoice, and settles those visits.
  7. If the patient wants another treatment, the cycle restarts from step 2 — a fresh consultation, possibly a different doctor — but it all keeps adding to the same patient's history. No new patient record is ever created for a returning patient.

The whole system rests on one rule: everything must be traceable back to patient_id — consultation, treatment, visit, prescription, or bill. That's why the patient page is built as tabs (Timeline / Consultations / Treatments / Billing) over one single record, rather than scattered screens.

Known gaps — not yet in the model

Checked against what typical dental practice management software covers; these are worth a decision before build.

  • GST fields on INVOICE. CGST/SGST split, GSTIN, HSN/SAC codes — Indian clinics generally need this, with therapeutic procedures often GST-exempt and cosmetic ones taxed.
  • Consent forms for procedures. RCT/extraction-type procedures typically need a recorded, signed consent, distinct from the clinical prescription notes.
  • Allergies & current medications as their own fields. Right now they'd sit inside the free-text medical_history field — worth splitting out so they can be checked before prescribing.
  • Recall / overdue-checkup list. An automation on top of appointments (once that exists) rather than a new entity — flags patients due for a follow-up.

Deferred to a later phase

Confirmed as needed eventually — not blocking the current build, since Admin/Doctor panels come first and the patient-facing side comes after.

  • Patient-facing login & appointment booking. No patient login in this phase. When the patient system gets integrated later, it brings appointment booking with it — a calendar, future slots, and (per Indian-clinic norms) WhatsApp/SMS reminders.
  • Doctor availability / scheduling. Seeing what each doctor is assigned to at what time, and when they're next free — rides on the same appointment system above, so it waits until that's built.
  • Capacitor-wrapped mobile app. Already accounted for in the system shape (same React/Vite build, wrapped by Capacitor) — just flagging it's a real deliverable for later, not dropped.
